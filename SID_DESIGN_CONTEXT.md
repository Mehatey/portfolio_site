# Sid Mehta portfolio: design context and reference map

> Canonical taste and intent brief for any AI or collaborator working on this
> portfolio. Read this before proposing, editing, generating imagery, or changing
> interactions. For implementation history and traps, also read `HANDOFF.md`.

## 1. What the site must do

This is a recruiter-facing portfolio for product design, visual design, brand,
design engineering, AI product work, and selected creative technology. It must make
Sid's value legible quickly without flattening the site into a generic case-study
template.

The site should communicate:

- Sid designs products and visual systems, then stays close enough to code to make
  the intended interaction survive.
- His strongest center of gravity is UI/UX, visual and brand design, and newer AI
  product work.
- Spatial and installation work matters, but should not imply that spatial design
  is his main specialty.
- He has range, but the recruiter path must remain obvious: who he is, what he did,
  what he owned, what changed, and where to go next.

The governing test: **memorable in five seconds, understandable in ten, credible
after a deeper read.**

## 2. Sid's taste

### Core qualities

- **Distinctive, atmospheric, and authored.** The site can feel like a world, not a
  résumé template.
- **Real over fake.** Prefer physically coherent lighting, reflection, refraction,
  depth, contact shadows, motion, and material behavior. Avoid effects that merely
  imitate those things with a gray blur or arbitrary glow.
- **Playful with consequence.** Hover, click, scroll, and easter eggs should reveal
  personality, meaning, or state. Ambient motion with no consequence becomes noise.
- **Personal.** Cube Guy, the cat, mandalas, music, photography, handwritten work,
  and humor can make the experience unmistakably Sid, provided they do not block
  the recruiter path.
- **Experimental but controlled.** One governing interaction or metaphor at a time.
  Strong silhouette and hierarchy before particles, distortion, or shaders.
- **Motion-forward, not motion-saturated.** Entry and scroll should feel authored,
  gradual, and causal. Avoid everything popping, dissolving, spinning, or animating
  at once.
- **Legible in light and dark.** Light mode is the default. Dark mode remains fully
  designed, not a color inversion.
- **Honest.** Say what shipped, what was solo, what was a pitch, what used mock data,
  and what failed. Never inflate years, metrics, ownership, or technical depth.

### Typography and layout

- Use a compact, disciplined type scale. No giant presentation-deck headlines.
- Do not use tiny uppercase labels as a substitute for hierarchy.
- Do not stack three or four caption tiers under every project.
- Align content to a clear grid and shared left edges. Sid notices optical drift,
  weak spacing rhythm, and things that appear to float without purpose.
- Favor one concise project statement plus one or two useful tags on indexes.
- Case-study openings need visible labels where they aid scanning: `Role`, `Client`,
  `Year` or `Duration`, optional `Team`. Links such as `Live`, `Award`, and
  `Publication` should form a separate compact row only when real.
- Use spacing, weight, contrast, and alignment before introducing another font size
  or card.
- Paragraphs need readable measure and decisive editing. Final work and outcome
  should appear before long process exposition.

### Color and material

- Never introduce arbitrary purple text. Purple has repeatedly read as generic AI
  styling rather than Sid's voice.
- Preserve the existing site color system and project art. Do not recolor original
  UI, award marks, logos, or final identities for visual consistency.
- Liquid glass should refract and define content behind it. A blurred gray slab is
  specifically rejected.
- Shadows need a believable source and contact. Avoid black blobs, muddy overlays,
  detached glows, and low-contrast text sitting inside effects.

## 3. Explicit rejection list

Do not propose these again without new evidence:

- Presentation layouts, pitch-deck cards, or huge type with small metadata beneath.
- Generic SaaS cards, Claude-like hierarchy, or templated AI portfolio styling.
- Purple accent text added without a project-specific reason.
- Polaroid frames for Play or photography.
- Pixelation on every hover, cursor captions following every card, or several
  simultaneous hover systems.
- Flat, unexplained code-themed copy. Code language is welcome only when it conveys
  real process clearly.
- Company-logo plaques. Simple type is preferred when company context is needed.
- Repeated `Solo`, year, role, and category labels that add no information.
- Award names rendered as awkward angled 3D text. Use recognizable official marks
  or quieter factual proof.
- 3D typography that is hard to read, sits on a black blob, or exists only as
  decoration.
- Unexplained galleries of process images. Every artifact must advance the story.
- Decorative objects added merely to fill the homepage scene.
- Effects that add permanent animation loops, poor responsiveness, or long loading
  without a meaningful payoff.

## 4. Homepage world

The homepage's water world is part of the identity. Preserve the mood, but make it
clearer and faster.

- Water should feel blue, reflective, alive, and materially coherent in light and
  dark themes. Avoid muddy brown or washed gray.
- Cube Guy and the cat can share a platform. The cat should feel intentional and
  respond naturally rather than perform a gimmick.
- Flora, fish, leaves, and small signs of life are welcome when restrained.
- Cube Guy hover should radiate or react with personality, not simply blur or scale.
- Award proof should remain recognizable and readable. Official Webby and Kyoorius
  marks are stronger than gold labels or steeply angled text.
- Hero copy should be compact, readable, and integrated into the composition. It
  should quickly convey product/design-engineering value without dominating half
  the screen.
- Header should be reliably visible. Homepage can keep résumé and LinkedIn visible;
  secondary pages may reveal them on nav hover if discoverability remains strong.
- Entry should tell a short story: essential sentence and character first, then
  surroundings. Avoid showing an old scene, black flash, dissolve, then sudden pop.
- Scroll into the next section should feel continuous, not like two unrelated
  presentations.
- Screensavers and perpetual shaders are expendable if they create lag.

## 5. Work, Play, Photos, and case studies

### Works

- Preserve all work. Curation and hierarchy are preferred over deletion.
- Recruiters should understand field, product, and proof without decoding multiple
  metadata systems.
- A project card should generally show: cover, project name, one concise descriptor,
  and one or two meaningful discipline/tool tags.
- Hover should clarify clickability through highlight, crop, border, or restrained
  motion. Avoid aggressive pixelation and cursor-following `View project` captions.

### Case-study opening

Above the fold should answer:

1. What is this?
2. What did Sid own?
3. Who was it for?
4. What result or proof exists?

Recommended composition:

- Project name.
- One memorable, honest one- or two-line premise.
- Final product or strongest outcome imagery.
- Compact labeled facts: role, client/company, year or duration, optional team.
- Contextual links: live product, award, publication, prototype, source.

Then show the final experience before process. Structure the body around decisions:
what was wrong, what was tried, why it failed, what changed, and what the evidence
shows. Avoid generic `Research / Ideation / Final UI` chapters unless those labels
carry real meaning.

### Play

Play is a working lab for:

- AI prototypes and agent interfaces.
- Games and interaction experiments.
- Creative code, installations, generative systems, and small technical studies.

Each entry should answer: what prompted it, what can someone try, and what Sid
learned. Strong entries can open into short write-ups. Play should not become a
random photo dump.

### Photos and video

Personal photography and short films deserve a separate, clearly named camera
archive. They demonstrate taste and observation without interrupting the design and
engineering sequence. Avoid Polaroid styling.

## 6. Interaction and sound preferences

- Interaction should feel magical but controlled, with a clear idle state and a
  short-lived response.
- A three-click easter egg was proposed: play a shimmer, emit cursor sparkles, show a
  brief personal fact or joke, and let a cube emerge as a temporary black-hole-like
  distortion. It must last roughly two or three seconds, respect reduced motion,
  clean itself up, and avoid a permanent animation loop.
- Potential rotating facts: current technology used, last song heard, recent travel,
  a phone/device Sid likes, a project animation detail, a meme/sticker, or the joke
  `I need / want a job`. Facts must come from a maintained source, never invention.
- Automatic piano/background music is rejected. Audio should be opt-in.

  reliably collapse and never leave a hand cursor/tool stranded.

## 7. Imagery direction

Follow `VISUAL_DIRECTION.md` for project-specific corrections. General rules:

- Prefer believable people using the product in the actual context.
- Favor candid framing, lived-in surfaces, selective focus, and natural background
  blur.
- Preserve exact UI and identity work. Do not redraw interfaces with generated text.
- Screens must have correct perspective, aspect ratio, glass contact, and reflection.
- People need plausible anatomy, scale, placement, and gaze.
- Avoid empty showroom mockups, universally sharp scenes, stretched screens,
  arbitrary branded props, and nested device frames.
- Produce one strong treatment first. Review image-first, then explain briefly.

## 8. Inspiration and what to borrow

The complete researched link library lives in `INSPIRATION.md`. Key references:

- [Joe Parker-Rees](https://www.joeparkerrees.com/) is the closest career and
  information-architecture reference. Borrow honest premises, final-work-first
  storytelling, decision-led process, and a clear relationship between Work and
  Playground. Do not copy his visual system, jokes, typography, or neutral cards.
- [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/) for contemporary interaction techniques.
- [Lusion](https://lusion.co), [Unseen](https://unseen.co/projects),
  [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com), [Active Theory](https://activetheory.net),
  and [Awwwards WebGL](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/webgl) for authored spatial
  interaction and performance-aware spectacle.
- CSS Design Awards 2025 winners studied in `INSPIRATION.md`: Symphony of Vines,
  Monolith Project, KOKUYO, Bruno Simon, and Merouane Bali.
- The useful lesson from award work: one governing metaphor, meaningful state
  changes, strong silhouette, authored camera language, consequential interaction,
  restraint, and performance designed into the visual system.

## 10. How an AI should work on this site

1. Read this file, `VISUAL_DIRECTION.md`, and the relevant sections of `HANDOFF.md`.
2. Inspect current code and live output. Historical audits are not ground truth.
3. Preserve unrelated dirty files and all existing projects.
4. Make the smallest coherent improvement that meaningfully helps recruiter clarity,
   visual craft, or performance.
5. Use real labels where they improve scanning. Do not confuse minimalism with
   removing information architecture.
6. Visual-QA desktop and mobile, light and dark, first load and scrolled state.
7. Check typography, alignment, measure, spacing rhythm, contrast, crop, hover,
   keyboard behavior, console, overflow, and performance.
8. Respect reduced motion and pause offscreen animation work.
9. Format touched files, build, stage only deliberate changes, commit, push, and
   verify CI.
10. Never claim visual QA was completed unless the rendered pages were actually
    inspected.

## 11. Portable north star

**Show the thing. Say what was real. Explain the decision. Keep the world memorable.
Let the recruiter go deeper without making them decode it.**
