# Voiceover scripts

Thirteen takes: one for the site, twelve for the prototypes on
`/ai-prototypes/`. Written to be spoken, not read, so they are short and the
sentences end early.

## How to record

- **Quiet room, phone or laptop mic held about a hand's width away, slightly
  off to the side** so plosives miss it. A closet with clothes in it beats a
  kitchen.
- **One take per script, then one more.** The second take is almost always the
  one. Do not edit mid take, just start the sentence again and cut later.
- **Leave two seconds of silence at the top and tail** of every file. That
  silence is what a noise reducer needs, and it gives the player somewhere to
  fade.
- **Read it slightly slower than feels natural.** Everyone rushes on tape.
- **Say the numbers as words.** "Fifteen hundred", not "one five zero zero".
- Save as `assets/audio/vo/<slug>.m4a`, matching the slugs in
  `_data/ai_prototypes.yml`: `feral`, `latent-atlas`, `mimic`, `guise`,
  `forecast`, `watcher`, `mirror`, `revision`, `vantage`, `flatten`,
  `amnesiac`, `captcha`. The site one is `intro`.

Each script is between fifty and seventy words, which lands around twenty five
seconds at a comfortable pace.

## 00 · intro

> I build things that run in your browser and think for themselves. No server,
> no API key, nothing phoning home. The model loads onto your machine and then
> it is yours. Some of these are useful. Some of them are arguments. All of
> them are meant to be opened and pulled apart rather than watched. Start
> anywhere.

_Delivery: this is the handshake. Warm, unhurried, a little dry on "some of
them are arguments"._

---

## 01 · Feral

> This is every animal sighting being logged on Earth, right now, turned into
> music. Real field recordings get woven into the synth voices, so what you are
> hearing is partly the actual animal. A small model learns the order life is
> being reported in and writes the lead line. Nobody composed this. It is just
> what today sounded like.

_Delivery: let "nobody composed this" sit for a beat._

---

## 02 · Latent Atlas

> Fifteen hundred works from the Met, arranged by nothing anybody wrote down.
> A vision model looked at each one and placed it near whatever it resembles,
> so you get drift instead of departments. Then you can type a phrase, storm at
> sea, and the camera flies to it. Not by tag. By what the picture actually
> looks like.

---

## 03 · Mimic

> Same live nature feed, sorted by sight instead of by name. Things cluster
> because they look alike, which is often not how they are classified. And when
> the feed comes in faster than the atlas can hold, it forgets: those hollow
> rings are shapes it expects to see but has not confirmed yet. Guesses,
> drawn honestly as guesses.

---

## 04 · Guise

> Look into the camera and it describes you, out loud, in its own words. Then
> it repaints you as whatever you ask for. Viking, marble statue, anything you
> type. There is a slider for how much of you survives the transformation,
> which turned out to be the interesting control. The keys live on a server, so
> nothing sensitive touches your browser.

---

## 05 · Forecast

> This reads the world's news volume as a tension field on the globe. A model
> trains itself on the last few hours, live, in this tab, and forecasts the
> next spike. Then it shows you how wrong it just was. That second part is the
> point. A prediction with no error next to it is decoration.

_Delivery: flat and factual until the last two sentences, which are the thesis._

---

## 06 · Watcher

> Live public street cameras, running through a real vision model in your
> browser, writing surveillance files on strangers. The inference is genuine.
> The threat scores and the dossiers are fiction, and they are confidently,
> constantly wrong. That gap is the piece. It is very easy to build something
> that sounds certain and is not, and it is being built everywhere.

_Delivery: this one is cold. Do not add warmth to it._

---

## 07 · Mirror

> Give it one line about a person and it builds a whole person back. Voice,
> face, history, opinions. Everyone here is invented, and none of it comes from
> a real stranger, because the point is not the data. The point is how little
> it needs before it commits. You can watch it decide who you are.

---

## 08 · Revision

> Every edit to Wikipedia, everywhere, as it happens. Each point of light is
> somebody changing the record this second. The middle shows you which article,
> how fast it is moving, and how often it is being undone. It is the largest
> thing we have ever written together, and it is never finished.

---

## 09 · Vantage

> A support agent has a reaction and a reply, and those are never the same
> sentence. Vantage gives it both. There is a private voice where it says what
> it actually thinks, and then the message that goes to the customer. Watching
> the distance between the two is the whole design question.

---

## 10 · Flatten

> Your webcam, turned into depth by a model that is guessing. Where it is sure,
> you get a room. Where it is not, the errors stretch into smears and ghosts,
> and I left all of them in. Cleaning those up would have hidden the one honest
> thing here, which is that it does not really know how far away you are.

---

## 11 · Amnesiac

> This is a real language model and it is losing its memory while you talk to
> it. Not a script, not a trick. The conversation history rots in the buffer
> and the model responds to whatever is left, so it starts confident and ends
> somewhere else. The picture behind it comes apart at the same rate.

_Delivery: gentle. It is sadder than it sounds on paper._

---

## 12 · CAPTCHA: After Human

> Ten tests, and the only proof you have is drawing. Fifteen seconds, torn
> paper, a pencil that responds to how fast you move. Then an AI answers the
> same prompt beside you and both drawings become objects. The last one asks
> you to redraw your first gesture from memory, which nothing else here can do,
> and neither can you.

_Delivery: the last clause is the joke and the point. Do not push it._
