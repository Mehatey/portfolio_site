{% comment %} ── THE MEDIA IS ON A SURFACE ──────────────────────────────────────────── Sid: "can we curve the scrolling images and the videos and blur them when they go up and come from down. like i had seen somewhere they have a webgl or shader in some portfolio so it feels like the media is alive and based on the scroll its curve changes." The portfolios he is describing put the whole page in a WebGL plane and sample the DOM into it. That is the wrong trade here: this grid holds fifty-seven live images and videos, and routing them through a texture atlas costs their crispness, their lazy decode, their text selection and every accessibility affordance they have, to buy an effect the browser can already do. So the surface is real 3D, not simulated. Each item sits on a cylinder: its distance from the middle of the screen becomes a rotateX about a shared perspective origin, so items lie back as they climb toward the top and tip forward as they come up from the bottom — the same geometry a cylinder gives you, without leaving the DOM. Blur and dim ride the same distance, so entering and leaving are one motion rather than three effects that happen to be near each other. THE PHYSICS is velocity, not position. A spring on position gives you bounce, and bounce on a grid this dense reads as jitter. Instead scroll SPEED adds curve and blur on top of the resting geometry and decays back at 0.9 a frame, so the surface tightens when you throw the page and relaxes when you stop. That is the part that feels alive: the grid is stiffer when you are moving fast through it. Usage: {% raw %}{% include scroll_curve.html selector=".play-grid figure" %}{% endraw %} {% endcomment %}
