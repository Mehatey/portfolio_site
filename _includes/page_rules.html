{% comment %} ── THE PAGE SAYS WHERE ITS EDGES ARE ──────────────────────────── Sid, with a screenshot of pear.no: "i was thinking whether it would help or look good on our site to have the side grid line like thing from the logo going horizontally and vertically down to structure people's eyes." Worth doing, and the reason is not decoration. Measured on /mool/ at 1440: the nav capsule and the logo sit at x=36. Every bleed, grid and caption on the same page starts at x=101. Two left edges, sixty-five pixels apart, and nothing on the page makes either of them look chosen. A reader does not measure that, but they feel a page that cannot say where its margin is. These rules are drawn on the edge the site already commits to — the nav capsule's own inset — so the logo's left edge lands exactly on the left rule, which is the "from the logo going down" part of what he asked for. Content stays inset from them, which is the same relationship pear.no has: their line runs between the mark and the headline, not through the headline. WHAT KEEPS IT FROM BECOMING NOISE This site is not pear.no. Pear can afford a full drafting grid because the page behind it is one flat painting and calm type; here there is a WebGL field, a water footer, curved media and a cube. So: · Two verticals and two horizontals. Not a grid — a frame. A grid of ten lines over a shader is texture, and texture is the one thing this site does not need more of. · They are drawn at 6% ink and never cross content, only sit behind it. · Plus-marks at the four intersections only. Pear puts a mark at every crossing because it has few crossings. · The whole frame fades out over the last stretch of the page, where the footer water takes the background over. A hairline ruled across a pond reads as a scratch on the screen. Fixed rather than scrolled: it is the window's frame, not the document's, so it stays still while the work moves through it. That is what makes it read as structure instead of as one more thing travelling up the page. {% endcomment %}
