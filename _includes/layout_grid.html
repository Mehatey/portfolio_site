Six vertical hairlines and three horizontal ones, fixed to the viewport, at the same gutter and column rhythm the content is laid out on. This is the oldest trick in editorial design and it does two things at once: it tells the reader the page was set rather than assembled, and it gives every full-bleed element -- the hero canvas, the pond, the wall -- something to be measured against, which is what makes a big empty area read as composed rather than as empty. They are FIXED, not scrolled, on purpose. A grid that scrolls with the content is a texture; a grid that stays is the frame the content moves inside, and the parallax between the two is most of the effect. CONTRAST BUDGET. 0.045 on ink. This has to sit under body copy on every route on the site, so the number is set at the point where the line is visible on a flat ground and invisible under a paragraph. Anything that reads as a noticeable line at a glance is too strong for something that appears on 40 pages. The horizontal set breathes with scroll velocity -- one shared --sva from scroll-velocity.js, no listener of its own -- so throwing the page separates the frame from the content for a moment and then lets it settle. {% endcomment %}
