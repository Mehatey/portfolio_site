{%- comment -%} ── THE QUICK VIEW ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Sid: "if the recruiter clicks on one of them, they have to go all the way to the bottom of the scroll, and they won't be able to see as many projects. The goal is for them to be able to quickly view different projects and see what else Siddharth is capable of." Measured, that instinct is right. Opening a case study costs a reader this much scrolling before they can judge anything: cube-guy 36 screens, mandalas 36, illustrations 31, alpha-stockathon and b-plus-b and aananda about 24 each. The shortest page on the site is marriott at 12. Nobody looking at eight portfolios in an afternoon pays that twice, so the first click is a commitment, and the cost of the commitment is that they see one project instead of eight. ── WHY NOT THE WHOLE PAGE IN A MODAL ─────────────────────────────────── The other idea on the table was opening the real case study inside a blurred sheet that grows. It was rejected for three reasons and they are all still true: 36 screens inside a modal is still 36 screens, so it does not solve the thing it was invented for; nested scrolling brings scroll chaining, trackpad momentum escaping the panel and mobile body-lock, which is a bug farm that behaves differently on the reader's machine than on ours; and /works/ is already 11.9MB, so holding a second heavy document open on top of it is the wrong trade on the page that has to feel fastest. What survives from that idea is the FEELING of the panel becoming the page, and that is what the expand control does -- a view transition into the real case study, with a real URL and a working Back button. Same language as hero_morph and case_lightbox, which already use startViewTransition. ── IT NEVER TRAPS ANYONE ─────────────────────────────────────────────── The cards stay ordinary links. Only an unmodified left click is intercepted, so cmd-click, middle click, right click and no-JS all still open the case study the normal way. That matters: a recruiter who opens six tabs is doing the exact thing this feature is for. The four images per project are curated in _data/works.yml under `quick`, not taken from the top of each page, because the top of a case study is intro and process. See the commit for how they were chosen. {%- endcomment -%}
