{% comment %} ── THE CUBE SAYS ONE THING ────────────────────────────────────────────── Sid: "people won't really click on it because they might mistake it as a part of a UI... it could be a friendly conversational piece... it doesn't open up the chatbot. it kind of just says it next to it." He is right about the diagnosis, and it is worse than a guess: a round button pinned to the bottom-right corner is the single most ignored element on the web. That is where Intercom and Drift live, and fifteen years of support widgets have trained people not to look there. The pulse does not help; pulsing is exactly what those widgets do. So the cube speaks first, once, and asks for nothing. THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS. Sid's standing note on this site is no narrator text -- no grey voice explaining a piece to the person already looking at it. We stripped that out of the art pieces. A bubble that says "here you will find twelve AI experiments" is that same narrator text with a tail drawn on it, and it would be the worst thing on the page. So: the cube OBSERVES, it never DESCRIBES. It is allowed to know something the visitor does not and be dry about it. It is not allowed to summarise the page. "Ten of these run on your GPU. Your laptop may get opinions." earns its place. "A collection of AI experiments" does not. Usage: {% raw %}{% include cube_says.html line="..." %}{% endraw %} Keep lines under about twelve words. The bubble is a beat, not a paragraph. {% endcomment %}
