# Portfolio imagery preferences

Saved from Siddharth's explicit feedback, September 8, 2026. Apply to future portfolio image work; later explicit feedback takes precedence.

## Photographic direction

Show the real intended user doing the real use case in a setting that fits the project. Prefer a candid product-in-use photograph with human presence over a pristine empty device display. Marriott: seated hotel associate using the tablet at reception, with naturally blurred guests in the lobby. Mool: believable Indian users and everyday Indian environments.

Use selective focus, background people softly out of focus, occasional natural motion blur, uneven available light, restrained reflections, subtle wear, and credible small signs of use. Keep the product clear. Avoid universal sharpness, showroom-perfect desks, sterile staged environments, generic AI decoration, and arbitrary backgrounds around a floating device. Do not add excessive dirt or clutter just to imply realism.

Preserve original UI, typography, logos, content, aspect ratios and device geometry. Map artwork to the physical surface accurately. Check screen corners, hand anatomy, contact shadows, finger occlusion, bindings, paper thickness and reflected light. Keep source artwork exact with compositing when generation changes it.

## Scope and review style

One strong treatment per request unless alternatives are explicitly requested. Image-first comparisons, minimal explanatory text. Inspect full-resolution details and desktop/mobile presentation. Keep originals, prompts and decisions. New applications are proposals, not evidence of actual client deliverables. Do not publish or redeem resets or incur separate API charges without explicit authorization.

Think beyond covers: useful product applications, packaging, menus, physical print, identity motion and narrative media. Each addition should explain the project to a recruiter. Keep each case study's visual language coherent; do not paste pitch-deck slides into a scroll story without adapting them.

### Screen mapping correction, September 8

Marriott's prior source crop contained an expanded navigation X, baked rounded outer screenshot corners and added black padding. User explicitly rejected this. Prefer the original tablet dashboard with compact navigation, extract only the active viewport, and map it flush to actual glass. Never nest a screenshot/device frame inside a second device. Preserve real component corner radii, but remove presentation-frame rounding and letterbox margins. Background people must have plausible scale, anatomy and placement; simply blurring conspicuous staged figures is insufficient.

### Background restraint and physical integration

For Marriott and similar product mockups, keep the product dominant. Prefer one distant blurred background person, at most two when useful. Avoid a conspicuous group or busy lobby. Foreground person using the product can remain. Screen mapping alone is insufficient: verify restrained glass reflection, believable bezel highlights, fingertip contact shadow and stand-to-desk shadow. No milky overlay or extra inset black framing.

### Tablet-first framing

User wants the tablet to occupy substantially more of the photograph. Reduce the visible hotel, departing guests, foreground head/shoulder and empty desk. Keep the complete device, interaction and believable context, but frame the tablet as the dominant product. Avoid repeating nearly identical wide environment shots when this tighter composition is requested.

Fairview cover: user also requests substantially more focus on the laptop. Make the device and actual interface dominant; retain people as secondary usage context rather than letting faces and kitchen occupy most of the cover.
